## Deviations from the command (and why)
- **H polar limit.** The crescent rule is applied only for |latitude| ≤ 66.56° (the polar circles). Beyond them some days have no sunset, so "the evening of the 29th" does not always exist, and the month-by-month chain drifted far from the moon (Tromsø 69.65° N: 164 months that were not 29/30 days; 75° N+: weeks to months off). There the app shows the Umm al-Qura date as before, without the "Predicted by crescent visibility" label. Stated in About (EN/BN), README and CHANGELOG.
- **H range.** The crescent chain starts at Muharram 1440 (Sept 2018) and runs to 2150 CE (the kernel's span); dates outside it, or with no saved location, fall back to Umm al-Qura, unlabelled.
- **About text.** One sentence added to Settings › About (EN/BN) for the moon-sighting / DE442S / tz-lookup credit (J) and one for the polar limit (H), so the app's claims stay honest.
- **G helper.** The time zone of the saved location is looked up offline with @photostructure/tz-lookup 11.8.0 (CC0), bundled; needed for G, credited in LICENSES/About.
- **Kernel start-up wait.** At start-up the app waits at most 15 seconds for the 31 MB kernel; if it is not ready by then the app starts with the Umm al-Qura date and switches to the crescent date when the kernel has loaded, so it can never hang on it (unit check).
- **Kernel source (L).** NAIF's server did not connect from the build box, so `de442s.bsp` was downloaded from a public mirror; its MD5 cc49327e06088124c0e39d8dde9f0b58 equals NAIF's official `aa_checksums.txt` (evidence-v3.0.6/kernel-checksum.txt).
